The Project

The challenge was to turn a student design into a running vehicle that could be judged on both engineering and on-track performance. That required the chassis, drivetrain, packaging, and fabrication work to develop together.

My contribution centered on the chassis and transmission. I designed the frame layout, worked through the gear, chain, and sprocket ratios, and supported the structural and aerodynamic evaluation before manufacturing.

02

My Contribution

The work connected calculations and simulation with the practical limits of building a competition vehicle as a team.

1

Chassis design: developed the frame structure around the driver, powertrain, and vehicle packaging requirements.

2

Transmission: calculated the gear, chain, and sprocket relationships used to define the kart's drive system.

3

Simulation: evaluated structural and aerodynamic behavior in ANSYS Workbench to support the design decisions.

4

Manufacturing: worked with the team to carry the design into a completed, competition-ready kart.

04

What I Took From It

This project made the relationship between analysis and fabrication very direct. A calculation or simulation only mattered if the team could turn it into a reliable part and integrate it with the rest of the vehicle.

  • Designed with manufacturing and packaging constraints in view.
  • Connected drivetrain calculations to a complete vehicle.
  • Used simulation as a design check rather than an isolated exercise.
  • Worked across disciplines in a time-bound team build.
