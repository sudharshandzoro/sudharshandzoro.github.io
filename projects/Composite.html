Composite Materials Research | September-December 2023

Composite Control Arm for Vehicle Suspension

An analytical study of replacing a conventional metal suspension control arm with an S-2 glass-fibre and epoxy laminate designed for strength, stiffness, manufacturability, and lower weight.

Reinforcement
AGY S-2 glass fibre
Matrix
CYCOM 890 RTM epoxy
Laminate
[03/904]S cross-ply
Manufacturing
Overbraiding concept

01

Engineering Objective

The control arm links the steering knuckle or wheel hub to the vehicle chassis. It must carry turning, acceleration, and vertical loads while maintaining wheel alignment and allowing suspension movement.

The project evaluated whether glass-fibre reinforced epoxy could replace steel or aluminum while improving corrosion resistance and weight efficiency. The concept also had to remain practical for a small production run of 10 to 100 parts.

02

Geometry and Load Cases

The control arm was approximated as connected plate regions between two chassis bushings and the wheel-side ball joint. Three vehicle conditions established the design loads.

1

Turning: a 10 km/h turn on a 15 m radius produced a calculated centripetal load of 665 N.

2

Acceleration: accelerating from 0 to 50 km/h in 5 seconds produced an in-plane load of 3608 N.

3

Vertical loading: the front-axle load produced 3188.25 N at one wheel and a calculated bending moment of 1291 Nm.

03

Design Inputs and Results

65%

Selected fibre volume fraction after comparing 50% and 65% material cases.

14 plies

Cross-ply laminate using 0.25 mm plies for a 3.5 mm laminate thickness.

1291 Nm

Calculated bending moment from the vertical wheel load.

0.346 mm

Calculated cantilever-style deflection for the bending case.

04

Material and Manufacturing Choice

AGY S-2 glass fibre was selected for its strength, availability, and comparatively accessible cost. CYCOM 890 RTM epoxy provided the matrix with moisture resistance and suitable mechanical properties.

Overbraiding was proposed for production because it can follow the control arm's curved geometry while supporting lower-volume manufacturing. The concept targeted an initial run of 10 test parts and a possible production quantity of up to 100.

The 65% fibre volume case produced stronger calculated stiffness properties than the 50% case and was carried into the laminate analysis.

05

Laminate Selection

Two stacking approaches were evaluated. The [03/904]S cross-ply sequence produced the preferred stiffness response and was simpler to manufacture than the angled-ply alternative.

The cross-ply sequence aligned the fibres with the primary control-arm directions. The analyzed in-plane load cases produced zero calculated shear strain, while the 0-degree outer plies improved bending stiffness.

For the higher acceleration load, the report calculated a larger longitudinal deformation than for the turning case. This reinforced the need to balance axial stiffness with the part's overall thickness and geometry.

06

Bending Configuration

The bending model added a core region between cross-ply skins, producing a 22-ply, 5.5 mm-thick analytical laminate for the vertical-load case.

The calculated bending stiffness matrix favored the [03/904]S arrangement over the angled-ply sequence evaluated in the study.

The analytical model predicted a 0.346 mm deflection under the assumed 3188.25 N wheel load.

07

Engineering Conclusion

The study established a feasible analytical direction for a composite suspension control arm using S-2 glass fibre, epoxy resin, and a manufacturable cross-ply laminate. The selected concept balanced stiffness, corrosion resistance, and production practicality.

  • The 65% fibre volume case produced the preferred calculated properties.
  • The cross-ply sequence simplified manufacturing and eliminated calculated in-plane shear strain.
  • Acceleration generated the larger in-plane stress case.
  • A skin-and-core configuration improved the bending response.
  • Physical prototyping and validation testing would be the next engineering step.
