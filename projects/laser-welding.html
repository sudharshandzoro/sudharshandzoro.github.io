Materials Research | January-May 2023

Laser Welding of Inconel 625 and Stainless Steel 316L

A Taguchi-designed experimental study of how laser power and welding speed affect penetration, tensile strength, hardness, and microstructure in dissimilar metal joints.

Materials
Inconel 625 / SS 316L
Method
Fibre-laser butt welding
Experiment
Taguchi L9 design
Testing
Tensile, hardness, microscopy

01

Engineering Question

Inconel 625 and stainless steel 316L combine corrosion resistance, strength, and heat resistance, making the material pair relevant to chemical processing, oil and gas, and nuclear applications. The challenge is producing a reliable joint without inadequate penetration, brittle phases, or excessive hardness gradients.

The study examined how fibre-laser power and travel speed change weld quality. The objective was to find a practical parameter combination that delivered full penetration, high tensile strength, controlled hardness, and a sound microstructure.

02

Experimental Plan

Nine weld conditions combined three power levels with three travel speeds. Each joint was evaluated through mechanical testing, optical microscopy, and statistical analysis.

1

Prepare 2 mm Inconel 625 and SS 316L butt-joint specimens.

2

Run nine fibre-laser welds using the Taguchi L9 parameter matrix.

3

Machine ASTM E8 tensile and microscopy specimens using wire EDM.

4

Measure tensile strength, Vickers hardness, penetration, and microstructure.

5

Use ANOVA and main-effects plots to identify influential parameters.

05

Parameter Effects

ANOVA showed that speed was the dominant factor, contributing 48.63% of the observed UTS variation. The interaction between speed and power contributed 27.27%, while power alone contributed 15.74%. The statistical model reported an R-squared value of 91.65%.

Fusion-zone hardness ranged from 210.7 HV to 321.7 HV across the nine conditions. Faster travel generally reduced fusion-zone hardness, while the Inconel heat-affected zone produced the highest local hardness values.

06

Microstructural Findings

Optical microscopy connected the mechanical results to grain structure and phase formation at the joint.

The weld structure transitioned from columnar dendrites near the base-metal interface to cellular dendrites toward the weld center as the temperature gradient decreased.

At the highest-speed conditions, martensite and Laves-phase formation, shrinkage voids, and inadequate penetration contributed to brittle failure at the stainless-steel side of the joint.

07

Engineering Conclusion

The work demonstrated that fibre-laser welding can produce a strong Inconel 625/SS 316L joint when heat input and travel speed are balanced. Welding speed had the clearest effect on penetration and joint strength, while excessive speed increased the risk of brittle microstructural features.

  • Lower speeds improved penetration and bonding.
  • High speed with insufficient heat produced incomplete penetration.
  • High heat combined with high speed promoted martensite and Laves phases.
  • Mechanical testing and microscopy supported the same failure mechanism.
